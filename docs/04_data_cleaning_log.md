# Data profiling & cleaning log

**Principle:** raw data is never modified (`data/raw/`, with SHA-256 hashes in `MANIFEST.json`). Every rule below is implemented twice, in `sql/load.sql` and in `python/olist_ba/clean.py`, and the two outputs are tested for equality. Rows are **flagged, not deleted**, unless a rule says otherwise.

## Source
- **Dataset:** Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist, version 2.
- **Page:** https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce
- **Licence:** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (confirmed from the Kaggle API metadata).
- **Size:** 9 CSV files, 126 MB uncompressed.
- **Coverage:** orders purchased from 2016-09-04 to 2018-10-17.

## Profiling results and decisions
| # | Finding (measured) | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly orders: 2016 is sparse (Dec 2016 has 1 order); Sep–Oct 2018 has 20 orders in total | Analysis window **Jan 2017 – Aug 2018** (20 complete months). 349 orders fall outside it and are excluded from trend KPIs. | Partial months distort trends and growth rates |
| 2 | `customer_id` is unique per order; 96,096 unique `customer_unique_id` across 99,441 orders | Customer-level metrics use `customer_unique_id` | Otherwise every customer looks like a one-time buyer |
| 3 | `review_id` has 814 duplicates; 547 orders have 2 or more reviews | PK = (review_id, order_id). Order-level score = latest answered review. | Keeps all source rows while giving one score per order |
| 4 | 775 orders have no items (603 unavailable, 164 canceled, 8 other) | Kept. They count in cancellation metrics and contribute R$0 revenue. | They are real orders that failed |
| 5 | 1 order has no payment record; 9 payments are R$0 (3 `not_defined`) | Kept and flagged in validation (INFO) | Immaterial; deleting them would break reconciliation |
| 6 | Payment total vs. item price + freight: 99.69% of orders match to R$0.01; 232 orders pay > R$1 more, 17 pay > R$1 less | Revenue KPIs use **item price** (product revenue); payments are reported separately | Differences are consistent with instalment interest and vouchers. Price is the cleanest revenue measure. |
| 7 | 1,359 orders: carrier handoff before payment approval. 23: delivery before carrier handoff. 14: status/date conflicts. | Flagged (`dq_*`). Excluded only from stage-duration medians (q08). | Likely timestamp-capture errors. They do not affect the late flag, which uses the purchase and delivery dates. |
| 8 | 8 orders `delivered` without a delivery date | `is_delivered` requires a delivery timestamp | Cannot assess lateness without a date |
| 9 | 610 products have no category; 2 categories have no English translation | NULL → `unknown`. Translations added: `pc_gamer`, `portable_kitchen_food_preparers`. Marked `is_translated_in_source = FALSE`. | Keeps 100% FK coverage; additions are transparent |
| 10 | Zip prefixes were stored as integers (leading zeros lost) | Left-pad to 5 characters | Required for geolocation joins |
| 11 | 451 customer cities have accents or special characters; casing is inconsistent | Lower-case, trim, strip accents | Consistent grouping |
| 12 | Geolocation has 1,000,163 rows, 261,831 exact duplicates, and points outside Brazil | Filter to Brazil's bounding box, then average per zip prefix (19,010 prefixes) | One centroid per zip prefix for distance estimates |
| 13 | Products have 2 rows with missing dimensions; the source has the column typo `lenght` | Kept NULL; renamed to `length` | — |
| 14 | `order_status` values are clean, but the code validates them | Unknown statuses raise an error (CHECK constraint / `ValueError`) | Fail fast on future data |

## Outliers
- **Prices** run from R$0.85 to R$6,735. **Freight** runs from R$0 to R$409.68. These are plausible for a marketplace (furniture, electronics) and are **kept**. Robust statistics are used where skew matters: medians for durations, Spearman for distance, Mann-Whitney for scores.
- **Delivery days:** P90 = 23.1 days, with a long tail. Kept. Lateness is measured against each order's own promise, so a long but on-time delivery is not penalised.
